Consumer Protection

Buy Genuine Gamet Bearings

Protecting your equipment starts with buying the right bearing from the right source. Learn how to identify fakes and where to buy safely.

How to Buy a Genuine or Fresh New Gamet Bearing

After inspection, Gamet's bearings are treated with a corrosion preventative oil (except pre-greased bearings). Initial packaging is heavy-gauge PVC bags, which are heat sealed and boxed. Bearings can be stored for long periods providing packaging is not damaged and the storage area is dry and not subject to extremes of temperature.

What to Look for When Buying

  • New HB / Gamet Packaging Since the 2019 acquisition by HB Bearings, genuine new stock uses the current HB Bearings / Gamet Bearings co-branded packaging. Older Gamet 600 Group packaging is distinctly different — verify the packaging matches the age and source of the bearing.
  • Batch Codes & Year Codes Batch and Year Codes used to be mentioned on older 600 Group bearings. On current Gamet bearings, only Batch Codes unique to the bearing number are mentioned. Any bearing claiming year codes in modern packaging should raise suspicion.
  • Current Logo vs Past Logo vs Fake Logos Gamet has used different logos across its history. The current HB Bearings / Gamet logo, the older standalone Gamet logo, and various fake logos all look superficially similar. Study the official logos carefully — fakes often have incorrect proportions, fonts, or spacing.
  • Fake Marking Style Gamet uses a very specific marking style on the cone and cup. Counterfeit bearings often have markings that are almost identical to originals but have subtle differences — incorrect font weight, differently spaced characters, or missing tolerances. Always cross-reference with official examples.

Beware of Counterfeit, Duplicate or Fake Gamet Bearings

Gamet has a distinctive marking style. Counterfeit bearings can appear very similar to originals — study these examples carefully to train your eye.

The marking on fake bearings is very similar to the original. Do not rely on visual inspection alone — always verify the source and request an Inspection Certificate.

STOP Usage of Second-Hand / Cleaned Bearings

Second-hand bearings that have been cleaned and repackaged are a major problem in the market. They look new but carry hidden damage, fatigue, and contamination that can cause catastrophic machine tool failure. Never reuse or buy used bearings — especially for precision spindle applications.
